# Structure — information architecture

**Read when:** naming things, laying out navigation, or deciding what lives
where.

- **Information architecture is a hierarchy of questions**, not a menu. Name
  each section by the question it answers.
- **Names are the product.** A wrong noun costs more than a wrong layout — it
  survives every redesign and every support conversation.
- **Depth over breadth fails; breadth over depth mostly wins.** Two clicks
  through clear labels beat one click through a guess.
- **Where am I, what can I do, what happens next** must be answerable at every
  moment without scrolling.
- **Consistency is a feature.** Two ways to do one thing is a bug, and one that
  spreads.

## Section headings

A heading states what the area *is* or what the user *can do*: "Selected KPIs",
"Plan status". Not a mood, not a brand line.

## Naming is design

`recentItems` versus `history` is a decision users feel. Pick the word the user
would use unprompted, then use it everywhere — on the screen, in the address, in the help, and
wherever the makers speak of it.
