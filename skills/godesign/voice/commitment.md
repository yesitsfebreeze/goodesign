# Commitment — the difference between correct and memorable

**Read when:** the work passes every check in `craft/` and still looks like
nothing. Read together with `voice/slop.md`.

## The moves

**Name a reference, take one thing.** A Swiss poster, an annual report, a
terminal, a museum wall label, a broadsheet, a control panel, a receipt. **One**
borrowed move — a rule, a scale, a way of setting numbers — carried through
consistently. Not a pastiche; a lineage. Write the reference into the brief so
it can be argued with.

**Type carries the personality, and it is the cheapest personality there is.** A
real display face, a real measure, real tracking, and one editorial move — a
hanging figure, an oversized rule, a caption set small and wide — does more than
any amount of colour.

**Asymmetry is a decision; centring is usually its absence.** Left-align the
hero. Let the right third stay empty on purpose.

**One distinctive move per screen, and it repeats across the product.** A
marker, a rule, a hairline, a numbering system, a single oversized element.
**One.** Two competing moves cancel out.

**Density is a position.** Airy is not automatically better. A dense, well-set
table is a stronger statement than the same data in cards.

**Where structure carries meaning, draw structure.** A drawn diagram beats a
stock illustration; the second is always decoration.

## The one constraint on being distinctive

Users spend most of their time in other products — **Jakob's law**. Keep the
**mechanics** conventional so the **expression** can be yours.

> Be strange in the type and the space. Never in where the close button lives.

## Trust

Every interface decision either builds or erodes trust, and it happens at the
pixel level. People can sense care and they can sense carelessness. Different
and new is relatively easy; genuinely better is very hard.

## The finish line

**Finished is when removing one more thing breaks it** — then one thing goes
back, deliberately.
